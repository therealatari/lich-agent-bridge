# Item identity and descriptions

Names, nouns, descriptions, and numeric game object IDs serve different purposes.
Two objects can share the same name, and one object's ID can change across
sessions or transformations.

Resolve an exact current object before an operation. Retain its observed
identity, location, descriptive fingerprint, and provenance in a private
dossier. An old database row is not proof of present ownership or accessibility.

Describe, inspect, analyze, and other attributed outputs may expose different
properties. Record which observation supports each claim; do not infer an
enchantment, charge count, hidden feature, or capability from appearance alone.

A closed container's remembered contents are historical evidence. Missing
objects in one display do not automatically mean they were lost, sold, or
removed. Keep unknown and absent distinct.

Tests should use unrelated synthetic equipment, quantities, and descriptions.
Renaming a real player's distinctive equipment does not make the fixture
non-private.
